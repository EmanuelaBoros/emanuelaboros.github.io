I study how machines read imperfect traces.

My work is on AI for difficult documents: receipts and insurance claims, epidemic reports, news and social media, historical newspapers and archives. I build methods to extract people, places, events and relations from them, and I measure what models miss, invent or flatten along the way.

    About

    Mostly serious work, with a sense of humour

    I care about what AI extracts from documents, and also about what it misses, invents, flattens, or quietly ruins while sounding confident.

    I come from both computer science and visual practice, so I tend to look at documents as text, images, layouts and traces all at once. My PhD was on neural methods for event extraction; since then I have worked on historical newspapers, epigraphy, document fraud, epidemic surveillance and open LLMs.

    Outside research: photography, handmade things, gardening with variable success, mostly non-fantasy books, metal festivals and camping.
