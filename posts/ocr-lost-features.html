Why one OCR mistake switches off a model's features

In plain words · November 2026 · about 4 minutes

OCRnamed entitiesinterpretability

When OCR gets one word wrong, the damage does not stay on that word. Here is what happens inside the model, one step at a time.

A named entity recogniser reads text and marks the people, places and organisations in it. Inside, it turns every word into a long list of numbers. We can rewrite that list as a row of features, think of them as lamps, where each lamp lights up for one pattern, such as “a digit three” or “a surname”. Press play and watch what an OCR error does to the lamps.

Toy numbers: here a word has 24 lamps and three or four are on. In the real model a word has 2,048 lamps and exactly 32 are on.

What to take from it

Counting wrong characters understates the cost of OCR. A misread word loses most of the features the model relied on, and a correctly spelled neighbour can lose some too. Those lost features are what I measure in my research, and the lamps that light up mostly on OCR junk can show where a page is damaged even when no clean text exists.
