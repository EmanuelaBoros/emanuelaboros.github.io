What is a named entity, and why is “Paris” hard?

In plain words · November 2026 · about 2 minutes

named entitiesentity linkinghistorical texts

People, places and organisations are the anchors of a text. Finding them is easy for us and surprisingly hard for a machine, especially in historical documents.

In a sentence like “the mayor of Lausanne met Mr. Dupont”, the words that point to specific real things, Lausanne and Dupont, are called named entities. Finding them is the first step in turning a pile of text into something you can ask questions of: who appears, where, and with whom?

Two separate problems

The first is recognising the name: this span of words is a person, that one is a place. The second is linking it: which Lausanne, which Dupont? Linking means connecting the mention to an entry in a knowledge base such as Wikidata.

Why history makes it harder

“Paris” can be a city in France, a city in Texas, or a person in a myth. Names were spelled differently, places changed their names or their borders, and many people mentioned in old newspapers have no entry anywhere. Add OCR errors on top, and a system has to guess from very little. A good system should also be able to say “I do not know”.
