What is a named entity, and why is “Paris” hard?

In plain words · November 2026 · about 4 minutes

named entitiesentity linkinghistorical texts

People, places and organisations are the anchors of a text. Finding them is easy for us and surprisingly hard for a machine, especially in historical documents.

In “Mr. Dupont left Paris for Lausanne in 1893”, some words point to specific real things. Those are named entities. Press play to see the two tasks a system has to do with them.

Toy scores for one illustrative sentence. Real systems rank thousands of candidates, but the logic is the same.

What to take from it

Recognising a name and linking it are separate problems, and each fails in its own way. Ambiguity, spelling variation, changing borders and OCR errors all push the same way in historical documents: less evidence, flatter scores. The useful property is not always getting the answer, it is knowing when the answer is uncertain.
