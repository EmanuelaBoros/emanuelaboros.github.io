Why a language model can sound sure and be wrong

In plain words · November 2026 · about 3 minutes

LLMsprovenanceuncertainty

Fluent text is not evidence. Here is how a model can repair a line beautifully and get it wrong.

A large language model is trained to write text that sounds like a good continuation of what came before. That is not the same as being right. Press play to watch it repair a damaged line.

Illustrative example with invented numbers. The names are made up for the demonstration.

What to take from it

A model makes the same confident noise when it reads a word and when it guesses one. In an archive that matters: a repaired name, date or place can change what the document seems to say. I ask of any system that it keeps the trace, shows what changed and stays checkable against the source. A fluent answer is a place to start checking, not to stop.
