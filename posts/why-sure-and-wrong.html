Why a language model can sound sure and be wrong

In plain words · November 2026 · about 2 minutes

LLMsprovenanceuncertainty

Fluent text is not evidence. A short note on why I like models that show where an answer comes from.

A large language model is trained to produce text that sounds like a good continuation of what came before. That is a different goal from being right. Most of the time the two go together. Sometimes they do not, and the model sounds exactly as confident in both cases.

The archive problem

In an archive, the difference matters. If a model “repairs” a damaged line of a newspaper, the result may read beautifully and still say something the page never said. It may change a name, modernise a spelling, or fill a gap with the most likely word instead of the true one.

What I ask of a system

Keep the trace. Show the original next to the correction, say what was changed, and keep a way to go back to the source. A fluent answer is a starting point for checking, not the end of it.
